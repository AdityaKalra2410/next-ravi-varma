Raja Ravi Varma painted the Indian epics in oil. What if he could paint any scene you describe? This is The Next Ravi Varma, by Team The Unsupervised.

=== Two inputs  |  starts 0:12.0  |  length 10.0 s ===
You type a scene from the Mahabharata or Ramayana, optionally add a photo of someone posing, and get a new painting in his oil style.

=== Four milestones  |  starts 0:22.0  |  length 10.0 s ===
Our project follows the four milestones of our proposal: the data, the style, the words, and the pose, all inside one web app.

=== Milestone 1 - Data  |  starts 0:32.0  |  length 22.0 s ===
Milestone one: the data. From 135 images, we kept only his 61 oil paintings, not the flat printed copies, and held nine out for testing. Qwen3-VL captioned every painting, and each caption starts with our trigger phrase: rrvarma oil painting.

=== Milestone 2 - Style  |  starts 0:54.0  |  length 22.0 s ===
Milestone two: the style. We trained a three-megabyte LoRA on frozen Stable Diffusion 1.5. Plain Stable Diffusion paints a photo, and asking for Ravi Varma gives a calendar print. Our LoRA paints in oil: style similarity 0.82, close to 0.84 for his real paintings.

=== Milestone 3 - Words  |  starts 1:16.0  |  length 14.0 s ===
Milestone three: the words. Qwen 2.5 expands a scene into a full prompt. A CLIP judge then picks the best of four paintings, raising scene match from 28 to 30.

=== Milestone 4 - Pose  |  starts 1:30.0  |  length 14.0 s ===
Milestone four: the pose. OpenPose turns a photo into a skeleton, and ControlNet paints a figure that follows it, with character looks, pose checks and a high-resolution refine.

=== Results  |  starts 1:44.0  |  length 12.0 s ===
Text alone gets poses wrong and adds extra people. With a pose photo, pose accuracy jumps from 10 to 97 percent, with no loss in scene match.
